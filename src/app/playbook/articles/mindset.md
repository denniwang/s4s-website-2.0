## Part 1: The Mindset Shift 🧠

If you’re reading this guide, chances are you have a decent idea of what colleges you would want to go to.
Maybe you even have a “dream” college (if you need help building a solid college list we got you—check out [this guide](https://example.com/guide)). Even if you aren’t super familiar with your options I’m sure you’ve heard of Harvard, Stanford, MIT, Princeton, etc. And 90% of you would lunge at the chance to attend one of these schools if you suddenly got invited. 
But why are we so obsessed with these names? What do you ***really*** know about these colleges, and what do you hope to get out of them (other than having “@harvard.edu” at the end of your email)?
The goal of this section is to answer this riddle, and to do so we're going to have our first action item of the guide. So grab a piece of paper (or open a google doc) and get ready to do some reflecting.

### why are you doing this, anyway?

📝 **Action Item**: Before you join another club, enter a competition, or open an application portal, hit pause. Seriously. Ask yourself:

- Writing a list of all the activities, clubs, and classes you’ve taken recently, which ones actually make you excited? Or at least, which ones do you not dread? What can you do to explore this excitement more?
- Now think about the activities you do that you ***don’t*** enjoy. Why do you still do them? Is it purely to put it on your college app?
- Finally, visualize your dream “college experience”, whether that’s strong academics, social life, unique clubs, or any of a billion things. Do the colleges you would kill to go to actually align with the activities you enjoy/not enjoy?

Hopefully you all can see the point of these questions. Too many of us treat college as the ultimate goal of our lives because we’ve had this idea drilled into our heads from our parents. *College should be an extension of the things you already love doing*. It’ll be hard to break out of the mentality that “prestige is everything” or “T20 or bust” but it’ll help you a ***lot*** in the long run. Stop doing things solely for college and start doing them because they interest you, excite you, incite curiosity, etc. This’ll make your life more enjoyable, less stressful, and actually increase your college prospects.
That’s a win-win-win.

📝 **Side Note**: There’s always going to be ***some*** stuff that you do solely for the college app. I didn’t take 15 APs in high school, for example, because I loved every single subject. I wanted to get the credits and slap those scores on my app.
The problem is when ***every single activity*** you do is because you’re trying to min-max the admissions game-—once you get into college, you’ll have no idea what to do for the next four years. *Do an activity **primarily** because you love it, and **secondarily** because it would look nice on your college app.*


### a word on prestige

“T10”. “Ivies”. “HYPSM”. The amount of ways that students refer to these schools proves just how much people love prestige. We just spent this section trying to shift away from this obsession, but that’s not to say that prestige isn’t important. 
Big name colleges open a lot of doors. Almost every student you meet at a big name school will be motivated, determined, and a good connection to have. These schools will have strong funding, and you’ll have a good first impression on employers. If you need a strong network and opportunities, these schools can make that easier.
But you can accomplish this anywhere else. It might take a little more digging on LinkedIn to find events and opportunities, but it’s still doable. 
The same way you factor in a school’s location, class size, and programs, you can evaluate prestige. *But it shouldn’t take special precedence. It’s just another factor.*

### where do we go from here?

Hopefully now you’ve accomplished the following:
- You’ve reflected on what activities you love, why you do the things you do, and maybe gotten a sense of where your passions lie.
- You’ve realized what qualities you want in a dream college, and have a better idea of what you’re working towards.

You should have a good idea of what you actually want to do, and maybe more generally, what it is you ***value***. From here, your efforts should be completely, totally, 100% concentrated on these values.

📝**Side Note:** Your passion right now does not have to be your passion in the future. Colleges know how unrealistic it is for a high schooler to find their life’s purpose. It’s ok to do something right now that you don’t necessarily want to do as your career, as long as you have the passion ❤️‍🔥🔥.
Plus, colleges *loooove* interdisciplinary activities. Some even ask you explicitly in their essays about how you’ve combined different fields. So if your passions are all over the place, find the common thread and start connecting them.

### tl;dr

College is just a means to an end. Let me say that again.
***College is just a means to an end***.
It’s easy to get caught up in the prestige, the competition, and forget what you’re actually working for.

The college application process demands dedication and resilience, but remember your inherent strength, vast capabilities, and amazing potential.

# 2 - The College App Timeline 📅

Last updated on July 25, 2025 • 10 min read

The college application process can feel overwhelming, but with proper planning and organization, you can navigate it successfully. This comprehensive timeline will help you stay on track and avoid last-minute stress.

### 9th grade: explore, and enjoy

[MENTOR_TIP: From a Harvard Grad | I tried 12 different clubs in freshman year and only stuck with 3. That's exactly what you should do - explore everything and don't feel guilty about quitting things that don't spark joy.]

In 9th grade you shouldn't even be thinking about college. Your only goal is to find out what you're passionate about and what you want to spend the next four years of your life working towards.
Here's our recommendation:

- **Explore:** Take advantage of club showcases and sign up for anything and everything that seems interesting.
  Signing up for a club is a zero-commitment activity. You can literally just not show up if you’re no longer interested.
  Your goal right now is to gather as much data as possible.
  Pay attention to flyers, school newspapers, and loud speaker announcements to find hidden opportunities.
  Try out for sports teams and competitions. You have nothing to lose!!
- **Connect:** Ask questions and be engaged in class to develop a relationship with teachers.
  Go to office hours and have casual conversations whenever you have the chance.
  Do it just for fun, with no incentive to get a letter of recommendation (that’ll be future you’s problem).
- **Have Fun:** Please, please, please don’t think about college too much. You just started high school!!
  Go make friends, talk to people, and try things out because you think it’ll be cool.
  Enjoy your time before junior year hits and you have to lock in.
- **Dream:** If you want, start casually researching diverse colleges. See what opportunities exist without self-imposing limitations.

### 10th grade: build a foundation

If 9th grade was about exploring, 10th grade is when you start cutting out the things you didn’t like and building upon the things you did like.

- **Build**: If you joined a club, apply for leadership. If you’re in a competition club, ask upperclassmen for the tips and tricks and see how well you can perform. Invest yourself more into your activities.
- **Plan**: As you build up your activities, start scanning through a list of majors and see if any of them align with your passions. No need to settle for anything. Just see what’s commonly available and whether any of those paths fit for you.
- **Study**: Most students take standardized tests (SAT/ACT) junior year. If you want the most stress free testing experience possible, start studying a whole year in advance. Just 10 minutes a day for one year is enough to crush either exam.
- **Visit**: Try visiting nearby colleges or taking virtual tours for your top picks. This will be your motivation for the next two years. Imagine yourself living on this campus, walking between classes, hanging out with all the cool college kids. And then lock in.
- **Summer**: Look into summer programs that relate to your interests. Colleges like MIT post lists of activities that they like to see, so use those as reference (but don’t limit yourself to anything).

### 11th grade: lock tf in

[MENTOR_TIP: Application Strategy | Junior year is when everything counts, but don't burn yourself out. Focus on depth over breadth - it's better to be exceptional at 2-3 things than mediocre at 10.]

Junior year … Everyone knows that this is where the grind truly starts. Between APs, SAT/ACT, and college, you'll have a lot on your plate. But it's totally doable! Just take things one step at a time:

- **Keep Building**: Just like in 10th grade, keep building on the same activities you’ve been working on.
  Found that club, become president, get 1st in the competition, climb up to Varsity.
  Whatever it is, do the best you can and have fun!
- **College List**: have a solid list of ~10 colleges ready to go. Read here for more.
- **Read Essay Prompts**: Most colleges rarely change their essay prompts.
  Even if they do, there’s so much overlap that planning ahead can only help.
  Go to the college’s portal and read this year’s essay prompts, loosely planning out what activities you can talk about.
  Just having the prompt in back of your mind can help you uncover great essay topics.
- **SAT/ACT**: If your colleges would benefit from test scores, now’s the time to sign up.
  You can take multiple attempts, so sign up as soon as you feel ready and don’t feel pressured to get it right the first time.
- **Letters of rec**: Before the year ends, ask for any letters of recommendation your colleges need.
  It’s best to get variety in perspectives, so ask teachers who have seen different qualities that you have.

# 5 - Waiting Game ⏱️️

Published on January 15, 2024 • 8 min read

The period between submitting applications and receiving decisions can be the most challenging.
But remember, you've successfully completed the hardest part.

### Story: Kai's Two Paths, One Happy Ending

Kai was waitlisted at his absolute dream school, a highly competitive East Coast university. The news stung deeply.
He immediately sent a heartfelt Letter of Continued Interest, detailing his latest academic awards and reaffirmed his unwavering commitment to their program.
Yet, he also wisely followed his counselor's advice: he shifted his focus.
He revisited the university that had given him a generous acceptance.
He joined their admitted student Discord channel, attended a virtual "day in the life" session for his intended major, and connected with a current student mentor.
Through this deeper engagement, he genuinely realized this accepted school had an incredibly vibrant community and innovative programs he hadn't fully appreciated.
By May 1st, the waitlist news from his dream school never came. And surprisingly, Kai no longer cared.
He had genuinely fallen for the school that had chosen him.
He proudly committed, understanding that the "right" path wasn't always the first one he envisioned, but the one he actively chose and enthusiastically embraced.
His journey taught him that true success isn't about gaining entry to one specific place, but about finding where you truly belong and can flourish.

### Understanding Admission Decisions

Receiving admission decisions can feel like opening a series of mystery boxes.
Knowing the various outcomes will help you decode them effectively.

**Decision Types:**

- **Accepted:** Congratulations! This is a tremendous achievement. However, maintain a balanced perspective until you've heard from all institutions on your list.
- **Denied:** This stings, and it's perfectly okay to feel disappointed.
  Crucially, remember that a denial is rarely a reflection of your worth or potential.
  Instead, it often means there were simply more highly talented applicants than available spots.
  Even exceptionally qualified students face rejections from top schools.
- **Waitlisted:** This decision indicates you are highly qualified and that the college likes you, but they do not currently have an available spot.
  A waitlist offer requires a strategic and proactive follow-up if you are serious about attending.
- **Deferred (from Early Round):** This means your application from an early decision or early action round has been moved into the regular decision pool for further consideration.
  This requires you to stay engaged and potentially send updates to reinforce your interest.

### Waitlist Strategies: Don't Give Up!

If your dream school has placed you on their waitlist, there is still a tangible possibility of admission.
A student waitlisted at five different schools, for instance, focused on Northwestern, gained admission, and graduated summa cum laude!

**Immediate Actions for Waitlisted Students:**

- **Accept Only if Serious:** Only accept a spot on the waitlist if you would genuinely enroll if offered admission.
- **Letter of Continued Interest (LOCI):** Send a sincere, concise letter of continued interest within two weeks of receiving the waitlist decision.
- **Provide Meaningful Updates:** Include any new significant achievements, awards, leadership roles, or substantial grade improvements since your initial application.
- **Reiterate Specific Fit:** Explain **why** you are still a perfect fit for **their** specific institution, referencing new programs or faculty that align with your evolving interests.
- **Secure Backup Plans:** Critically, ensure you have secured an acceptance and a viable plan at another school where you would be happy and successful.

⚠️ **Reality Check:** Waitlist acceptance rates can fluctuate wildly year to year and school to school.
At highly selective institutions, the acceptance rate from the waitlist can be less than 5%.
Maintain hope, but also a healthy dose of realism.

### Handling Rejection: It's NOT Personal

Receiving a rejection is undeniably tough, and it's perfectly valid to feel disappointed or even frustrated.
However, it's crucial to remember that the college application process is intensely competitive – often described as a "bloodbath" – for **everyone**.

**Healthy Processing Strategies:**

- **Acknowledge Your Feelings:** Allow yourself to genuinely feel the disappointment without suppressing it.
- **Seek Support:** Lean on trusted friends, family members, or school counselors.
  Talking through your emotions can provide perspective and comfort.
- **Cultivate Perspective:** Understand that rejection is rarely personal. It frequently means that, at that specific moment, you weren't the right "mutual fit" for that institution, or simply that there weren't enough spots for all the exceptionally talented applicants.
- **Re-Focus Forward:** Consciously shift your energy and excitement towards the compelling opportunities available at the schools that **did** offer you admission.
- **Consider Transfers:** Remember that many "dream schools" can sometimes be accessed via the transfer route after a successful year or two at another institution.
  The door is rarely permanently closed.

### Demonstrated Interest: Stay Engaged in Spring

Once admission decisions begin to roll in, maintain active engagement with the schools you are seriously considering.
This continued "love" reinforces your commitment and helps finalize your decision.

**Spring Actions for Admitted Students:**

- **Attend Admitted Student Days:** If possible, participate in these events, either in person or virtually.
  These days are specifically designed to "woo" admitted students and provide deeper insights into campus life.
- **Connect with Current Students:** Reach out to students currently enrolled in your potential major or extracurricular activities.
  Their experiences offer invaluable real-world perspectives.
- **Join Social Groups:** Engage with future classmates in accepted student social media groups.
  This helps build community and familiar faces before you even arrive.
- **Final Visit (If Applicable):** If you haven't yet visited, or if a specific school is a top contender, a final campus visit can be instrumental in solidifying your decision.
- **Maintain Communication:** Keep the admissions office updated with any new achievements or questions you may have.

### Making Your Final Choice: Trust Your Gut

This is an exciting culmination of your hard work: decision time!

**Decision-Making Framework:**

- **Create a Comparison Chart:** Seriously, build one. List all your accepted schools side-by-side and systematically weigh the following factors:
  - **Academics (30% weight):** Program strength, research, faculty, course offerings.
  - **Financial Fit (25% weight):** Net price, total debt, scholarship renewal.
  - **Campus Culture (20% weight):** Social scene, student values, community feel, support systems.
  - **Location/Environment (15% weight):** City, suburban, rural; climate; proximity to home.
  - **Career Services/Outcomes (10% weight):** Internship opportunities, alumni network, job placement rates.
- **Trust Your Gut Feeling:** After all the logical research and comparisons, where do you **feel** you will genuinely thrive, be happy, and grow the most?
  This intuitive sense is powerful.
- **Consider the Whole Picture:** Beyond the percentage breakdown, reflect on:
  - What will your total student loan debt be upon graduation?
  - How strong and reputable is the program in your **specific** field of interest?
  - What mental health and comprehensive support resources are readily available?
  - What internship, research, or hands-on learning opportunities are unique to that institution?
  - How robust and supportive is the alumni network for future career development?

**Decision Day Moves:**

- **Don't Hesitate:** If you are 100% confident in your choice before the May 1st deadline, submit your commitment!
- **Decline Respectfully:** Inform other colleges that offered you admission that you will not be attending.
  This is a professional courtesy and frees up spots for other students.
- **Submit Your Deposit:** Ensure your enrollment deposit is submitted by the deadline to secure your place.
- **CELEBRATE!** You have earned this moment. Take time to acknowledge your hard work and success.
- **Connect with Classmates:** Begin engaging with your future classmates through official university groups or social media.
